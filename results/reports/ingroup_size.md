# Ingroup-size sweep benchmark (ARGBasedInference, n_out=0)

Per-bin SFS accuracy and overall posterior confidence as a function
of the number of ingroup haplotypes available to the Felsenstein
kernel in **ARG ingroup-only mode** (n_out=0). One large simulated
ARG; each cell randomly subsamples the ingroup down to ``n_ingroup``
with a per-cell RNG seed for noise bars (3 seeds per n_ingroup).
``JC69 + uniform prior + full`` throughout.

**Folded SFS bin** = ``min(derived_count, n_ingroup - derived_count)``
where ``derived_count`` is computed against the simulator's truth
ancestral allele. Each n_ingroup contributes folded bins 1..⌊n/2⌋;
missing bins show as ``--``.

**Simulator settings**

- ``n_ingroup_max`` = 1000
- ``length`` = 1000000.0
- ``mu`` = 1.25e-08
- ``rec_rate`` = 1e-08
- ``pop_size`` = 30000.0
- ``seed`` = 42
- ``n_ingroups`` = [20, 50, 100, 200, 500, 1000]
- ``seeds`` = [100, 101, 102]

## Baseline-MAP agreement (n/a)

B7 is the ingroup-only sweep (n_out=0 by design). The B11
``MajorityOutgroupInference`` baseline requires at least one
outgroup observation per site to vote on the ancestral allele, so
the agreement column is undefined for this benchmark. The cells'
per-site JSON intentionally omits ``baseline_map``; the B6/B8/B9
reports carry it where outgroups are available.

## Reading the trend

- The headline question: does scaling up the ingroup beyond B6's
  ``n=20`` cell actually help ARG ingroup-only mode polarise sites?
  Compare the row at folded bin 1 (singletons) across the n_ingroup
  columns — that bin carries the least ingroup signal and benefits
  most from outgroups in B6.
- If accuracy at each folded bin is roughly flat across n_ingroup,
  the conclusion is that adding ingroup haplotypes mostly grows the
  *catalog* of low-frequency sites rather than improving per-site
  identifiability — i.e. ingroup-only mode is indeed information-
  limited by the ingroup MRCA depth, not by sample size.
- If accuracy at fixed bins climbs with n_ingroup, then larger
  ingroups do shift the ingroup-MRCA toward the deep ancestor enough
  for the kernel to recover more truth alleles — i.e. n=20 was just
  too small in B6.
